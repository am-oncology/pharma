Hard flags

Events that end a thesis outright: clinical holds, complete response letters, failed primary endpoints, terminated or suspended registrations, going-concern language. These override the composite score entirely. No momentum reading outvotes a clinical hold.

How the score is built

Ten factors are computed for every eligible name, standardised across the universe as z-scores, weighted, and summed. The factor bars on each card show every contribution to that total, so any ranking can be taken apart and argued with.

Price factors

Relative strength against XBI over one and three months; position against the 50-day and 200-day moving averages; six-month absolute return; and an accumulation measure (the share of recent volume transacted on advancing days, scaled by how unusual that volume is). Stretched RSI is penalised in both directions.

Registry factors

ClinicalTrials.gov is snapshotted on every run and diffed against the previous one. A status flipping from recruiting to active-not-recruiting means enrolment closed and a readout is on the clock. A primary completion date sliding right is a delay that will usually be acknowledged publicly much later, if at all. None of this is privileged: it is a public registry that few people diff.

Financing factors

Cash and quarterly operating burn are read from XBRL company facts on data.sec.gov and turned into a runway estimate. A company with nine months of cash and a readout eleven months out will raise equity before the data arrive. Recent 424B5 and S-3 filings add to the same risk score.

News factors

A weighted keyword lexicon with a seven-day half-life, run over trade press, regulator feeds and journal tables of contents. It is a lexicon rather than a neural sentiment model on purpose: general-purpose models read "Phase III trial fails to meet primary endpoint" as mildly negative prose when it is a company-ending event, and a lexicon can be inspected and corrected when it is wrong.

What this cannot do

  • It cannot read a hazard ratio. The lexicon will tell you a readout happened. Whether an HR of 0.87 in that population is commercially meaningful is a clinical judgement, and it is the part you are actually good at.
  • Momentum barely applies here. Small and mid-cap biotech prices are step functions punctuated by binary readouts, not diffusion processes. The Gaps column counts single-day moves above 15% in the past year; where that number is high, read the momentum factors as weak evidence.
  • Backtests on this universe will lie to you. Price history is served for currently listed tickers only. Companies that were acquired, delisted or went bankrupt are simply absent, and biotech has an unusually high rate of all three. Any historical study built on a universe assembled today will look far better than reality.
  • Catalyst proximity is variance, not direction. An imminent Phase III readout means something large is about to happen. It does not mean it will be good.
  • The data are stale by design. GitHub Actions runs on a schedule, so figures are typically 15–60 minutes old. Fine for catalyst-driven work; useless intraday.

Before you trade on any of this

If you work in clinical practice or research, check your employer's conflict-of-interest policy first. Being an investigator on a trial, sitting on an advisory board, or seeing embargoed conference abstracts or unblinded data can make trading in the sponsor a market-abuse offence under UK MAR regardless of intent. The excluded.tickers list in universe.json exists for exactly this: names on it are ingested and displayed but never ranked or recommended. Populate it before you rely on the board.
